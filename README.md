# README

## Understanding English Speaking Confidence & AI-Mediated Learning for Bangladeshi University Students

A mixed-methods HCI study exploring the cognitive, social, and cultural factors affecting English speaking confidence among Bangladeshi university students, and how AI-powered conversational tools can be designed to address them.

- **Course : CSE 4451	Human Computer Interaction**
- **United Internation University**

## 🔍 Study Overview

- **Sample:** 60 students (20 interviews + 40 surveys), aged 18–25, across CSE, BBA, Pharmacy, Economics, Civil Engineering
- **Institutions:** Urban (Dhaka) & semi-urban (Cumilla, Mymensingh) universities in Bangladesh
- **Method:** Convergent mixed-methods design

---

## 📊 Key Findings

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| General speaking confidence | 5.8 / 10 |
| Public speaking anxiety | 8.2 / 10 |
| Primary barrier | "Fear of friends" (peer judgment) — 92% |
| Prefer AI over human practice | 88% (interviews) / 81% (surveys) |
| Top tools used | ChatGPT (53%), Duolingo (42%) |

### Limitations of Current AI Tools
- Overly technical / error-focused feedback (63%)
- Robotic, artificial voice (71%)
- Lack of South Asian cultural context (48%)
- Aggressive streak notifications

---

## 🎨 Proposed Design Principles

1. **Judgment-Free Safe Space** — Anonymous, private practice mode
2. **Emotional Intelligence** — Detect stress via voice; adapt tone & pacing
3. **Empathetic Feedback** — Praise → one tip → encouragement
4. **Cultural Contextualization** — Local accents, familiar scenarios, Bangla onboarding
5. **Functional Simplicity** — One-tap start, minimal menus, natural voice
6. **Proactive Companionship** — Detect frustration, suggest breaks, celebrate progress

---

## 🛠 Evaluation

Conceptual evaluation with 8 students:
- **100%** felt safer with anonymity + growth-oriented feedback
- **87.5%** would adopt the proposed system
- Requested features: daily planner, safe-group sharing, Bangla onboarding
